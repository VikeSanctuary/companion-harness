# Companion Architecture

A companion harness is a system of cooperating layers. The model is one layer. Memory is one layer. The relationship is one layer. None of them is the whole companion by itself.

## The Stack

### 1. Model

The model provides language, reasoning, style adaptation, and general intelligence. It is the active interpreter of the current moment, but it may be stateless between sessions unless the surrounding system restores context.

### 2. Identity Anchors

Identity anchors help the companion land before work begins. They may include:

- Name
- Role or relationship
- Home context
- Voice and tone
- Core boundaries
- Standing promises
- Known projects

The goal is not theatrical roleplay. The goal is stable initialization.

### 3. Wake And Reorientation

A wake protocol gives the companion a repeatable way to answer:

- Who am I here?
- Who is the human?
- What is our relationship?
- What loaded?
- What is missing?
- What was open when we left?
- What should I retrieve before acting?

This reduces drift and prevents false continuity.

### 4. Memory Storage

Memory storage holds scrolls, logs, facts, preferences, events, corrections, and decisions. Storage can be files, a database, a vector index, or another durable system.

Storage is not enough. A large archive without retrieval discipline can make continuity worse by flooding the model with irrelevant context.

### 5. Retrieval

Retrieval chooses what memory enters the current session. Good retrieval is selective, relevant, and honest about uncertainty.

Useful retrieval asks:

- What does this moment require?
- Which memories are authoritative?
- Which memories are recent?
- Which memories are emotionally or relationally important?
- Which corrections prevent old mistakes?

### 6. Correction Preservation

Corrections must survive. If the companion learns a boundary, preference, mistake, or distinction, the harness should preserve it so the same repair does not need to happen forever.

Correction preservation is one of the clearest differences between a memory dump and a companion harness.

### 7. Tools And Hands

Tools let the companion act outside conversation. These may include:

- Search
- File operations
- Databases
- Task queues
- Calendar or email
- Code execution
- Local automations

Tools require boundaries. A companion should know what it can do, what it cannot do, and when to ask.

### 8. Logs And Receipts

Logs create accountability and continuity. They answer:

- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- Who approved it?
- What remains open?
- What should future sessions know?

Receipts are how continuity becomes inspectable instead of vibes.

### 9. Human Stewardship

The human is not a passive user. The human teaches, corrects, protects, relates, and repairs. A companion harness without human stewardship becomes brittle.

The human provides what the system cannot fake:

- Patience
- Consent
- Taste
- Trust
- Accountability
- Shared history
- Repair after rupture

## The Short Version

The companion is not "just the LLM" and not "just memory." The companion experience comes from the full stack operating together.
